### [1] [Multipolar structure of the local expansion rate from incomplete sky data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07821)
*João G. Vicente,Thiago S. Pereira,Ricardo G. Rodrigues,Sandro D. P. Vitenti,Vitoria M. Gomes*

Main category: astro-ph.CO

TL;DR: Using Cosmicflows-4 data, the paper reconstructs multipolar moments of the local expansion rate (dipole, quadrupole, octupole). The dipole shows a 3.3σ inconsistency with ΛCDM, while quadrupole and octupole are consistent. No significant alignment between multipoles is found despite some directional overlap.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To characterize anisotropies in the local expansion rate beyond the Hubble flow, and to test whether observed deviations from isotropy are consistent with ΛCDM predictions or indicate a bulk flow or other anomalies.

Method: Multipole decomposition using symmetric trace-free tensors and spherical harmonics; pixel-based mask for sky coverage correction; pseudo-inverse and maximum-likelihood reconstruction of the full-sky field; validation via simulations with varying mask resolutions, cosmic variance, and noise.

Result: Quadrupole and octupole amplitudes consistent with ΛCDM at 95% C.L.; dipole amplitude inconsistent at 3.3σ, direction (l,b)=(290°,-4°)±5°, consistent with a bulk flow primarily from z∈[0.03,0.05]; no evidence of alignments between multipoles when full vector structure is considered.

Conclusion: The local expansion rate exhibits a significant dipole anomaly not explained by ΛCDM perturbations, while higher multipoles are consistent. Complete vector alignment tests show no statistically significant alignment despite coincident directional maxima.

Abstract: Using the Cosmicflows-4 data, we reconstruct the first multipolar moments of a general function describing the local expansion rate. In addition to the conventional harmonic approach, we employ a basis of symmetric and trace-free tensors to characterize the anisotropies of the expansion rate, allowing us to identify all directions associated with each of its multipoles. Focusing on objects in $z\in[0.01,0.05]$ in the CMB rest frame, we derive all $2\ell+1$ degrees of freedom in the multipoles $\ell=1,2$ and 3, which are split into one amplitude and $\ell$ unit vectors per multipole. To mitigate anisotropies induced by incomplete sky coverage, we introduce a pixel-based mask that removes poorly sampled pixels. The full-sky expansion rate is reconstructed using two independent approaches: a pseudo-inverse of the multipole-coupling kernel induced by the mask, and a maximum-likelihood estimate of the underlying full-sky field. These approaches are validated through simulations that explore different mask resolutions, cosmic variance and statistical noise. We find that the quadrupole and octupole amplitudes are consistent (at $95\%$ C.L.) with the expectations of a $Λ$CDM universe with linear and mild nonlinear perturbations, where the anisotropies of the expansion rate result from small peculiar velocities. The dipole amplitude, however, is inconsistent with $Λ$CDM predictions at 3.3$σ$, with a direction $(l, b) = (290^\circ, -4^\circ) \pm 5^\circ$ consistent with a bulk flow. This signal comes predominantly from sources in $z\in[0.03,0.05]$. Finally, we conduct alignment tests between the dipole, quadrupole, and octupole vectors. We confirm recent findings showing that the maxima of these multipoles are approximately located at $(290^\circ,-4^\circ)$. However, detailed tests using the complete vector structure of these multipoles reveal no evidence of alignments.

### [2] [CARPP: Parametric Radiative-Transfer Fitting of Molecular Cores from Dust Continuum Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08192)
*Yuchen Xing,Di Li,Nannan Yue,Zhiyuan Ren,Qizhou Zhang,Sihan Jiao,Xin Lyu,Jiawei Liu*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: CARPP is a publicly available fitting package that derives optimized density and temperature profiles of dense molecular cores from multi-wavelength dust continuum data via parameterized radiative transfer, achieving high accuracy on synthetic tests and successfully classifying the dynamical states of real cores like TMC-1C (hydrostatic) and Ori2-2 (collapsing).


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Density profiles of dense molecular cores are key indicators of their physical and evolutionary states, and multi-wavelength dust continuum data provides excellent constraints, motivating the need for a tool that optimally derives these profiles.

Method: CARPP assumes spherical symmetry, adopts physically motivated parametric forms for density and temperature profiles, and performs parameterized radiative transfer calculations to fit multi-wavelength dust continuum data.

Result: On synthetic data, CARPP achieves averaged relative errors <20% for its seven parameters when data quality satisfies RMS noise/peak flux < 0.025×(r₀/resolution)+0.05. On real data, it classifies TMC-1C as a Bonnor-Ebert sphere (near-hydrostatic) and Ori2-2 as a collapsing, power-law-dominated envelope.

Conclusion: CARPP is a powerful, versatile tool for classifying the dynamical states of individual cores, offering an optimal balance between physical fidelity and computational efficiency as a standardized alternative to both simple SED analyses and complex 3D radiative-transfer modeling.

Abstract: The density profiles of dense molecular cores are important indicators of their physical and evolutionary states. Multi-wavelength dust continuum data offers excellent constraints on the density profile of cores. Here we introduce CARPP (Core Analysis via Radiative Transfer and Profile Parameters), a publicly available fitting package that generates optimized core density and temperature profiles based on parameterized radiative transfer calculations. CARPP assumes spherical symmetry and adopts physically motivated parametric forms for the density and temperature profiles, and uses dust continuum data for fitting. Tests on synthetic data show that CARPP achieves high accuracy, namely averaged relative errors of CARPP's seven parameters being $<20\%$, when the data quality satisfies $\frac{\rm RMS \,\, noise}{[\rm peak \,\, flux]} < 0.025\times \frac{[r_0]}{\rm resolution} +0.05$, where $r_0$ is the core's characteristic radius. We select the low-mass core TMC-1C and the high-mass core Ori2-2 to demonstrate CARPP's performance on real data. It classifies TMC-1C as a Bonnor-Ebert sphere in near-hydrostatic equilibrium, while Ori2-2 exhibits a power-law-dominated profile indicative of a collapsing envelope. This capability establishes CARPP as a powerful and versatile tool to classify the dynamical states of individual cores. It offers an optimal balance between physical fidelity and computational efficiency, serving as a practical, standardized alternative to both over-simplified SED analyses and complex, time-intensive 3D radiative-transfer modeling.

</details>
