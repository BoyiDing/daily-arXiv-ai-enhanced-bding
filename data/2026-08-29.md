### [1] [Scatter, bias, and chaos of satellite orbits in triaxial dark matter haloes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26249)
*Barry T. Chiang,Frank C. van den Bosch,Michael A. Keim*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper quantifies the errors in satellite galaxy orbital calculations that arise from assuming spherical host dark matter haloes when haloes are actually triaxial. Using 10^6 simulated satellites in NFW hosts of varying triaxiality, the authors find that triaxiality leaves population medians unchanged but causes successive pericentre distances of individual orbits to scatter by 5-23%, systematically reduces minimum pericentre by up to 21%, and enhances tidal mass loss. Orbital dephasing is mostly regular (not chaotic). Spherical reconstruction via backward integration diverges by ~0.1 virial radius within 2-4 Gyr. For the Milky Way dwarf Triangulum II, host shape and orientation uncertainties alone spread its inferred pericentre by ~50% — five times the reported uncertainty and more than ten times the LMC-induced shift — biasing it by 6-70%. The host-shape uncertainty can dominate satellite orbital error budgets and should be included in future analyses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Satellite galaxy orbits govern tidal stripping, quenching, and survival, but are almost universally computed assuming spherical host potentials, despite dark matter haloes being generically triaxial. The magnitude and character of the error this simplification introduces has not been systematically quantified orbit by orbit.

Method: The authors integrate 10^6 satellite orbits drawn from a cosmological infall distribution in static NFW host potentials with systematically varying triaxiality, holding the mass profile fixed. They compare orbital parameters (pericentre, apocentre, period) across host shapes, classify orbital dephasing as regular vs. chaotic, test spherical reconstruction via backward integration, and apply the formalism to a specific case study (Triangulum II in the Milky Way).

Result: Population medians of pericentre, apocentre, and period are essentially unchanged by triaxiality, but individual orbits show successive pericentre scatter of 5-23%, with minimum pericentre shrinking by up to 21%, enhancing peak tidal mass loss. Dephasing is predominantly regular rather than chaotic; chaotic orbits are common and dominate near-centre pericentric passages in flattened hosts but diverge on timescales far exceeding the Hubble time. Reconstructed orbits in a triaxial host diverge by a tenth of the virial radius within 2-4 Gyr, with comparable contributions from unknown host shape and orientation. For Triangulum II, these unknowns alone spread the inferred pericentre by ~50% (vs. ~10% reported uncertainty and <5% LMC shift), biasing it by 6-70%.

Conclusion: Host-halo triaxiality (shape and orientation) introduces uncertainties that can dominate satellite orbital error budgets and largely exceed conventionally quoted uncertainties (e.g., the LMC perturbation). Orbital inferences should incorporate host-shape uncertainty as a standard component of the error budget.

Abstract: The pericentric distances of satellite galaxies govern their tidal stripping, quenching, and survival, yet their orbits are almost universally computed in spherical host potentials, whereas dark matter haloes are generically triaxial. We quantify, orbit by orbit, the error this simplification incurs. We integrate $10^6$ satellites, drawn from a cosmological infall distribution, in static NFW hosts of systematically varying triaxiality at fixed mass profile. Triaxiality leaves the population medians of pericentre, apocentre, and orbital period essentially unchanged. Instead, successive pericentres of an individual orbit scatter by 5-23% depending on host shape, irrespective of orbital energy; the minimum pericentre distance reached within a Hubble time shrinks systematically by up to 21%, enhancing the peak tidal mass loss. This triaxiality-driven orbital dephasing is overwhelmingly regular rather than chaotic; chaotic satellite orbits are common and dominate the near-centre pericentric passages in strongly flattened hosts, but diverge on time-scales far exceeding the Hubble time. Satellite orbits spherically reconstructed via direct backward integration in a truly triaxial host diverge by a tenth of the virial radius within 2-4 Gyr, with comparable uncertainties sourced separately by the unconstrained shape of the host and by its unknown absolute orientation. For the Milky Way dwarf Triangulum II, the unknown halo shape and orientation alone spread the inferred pericentre by ~50%, five times its reported uncertainty and more than ten times the LMC-induced shift, and bias it by 6-70%. This host-shape uncertainty can dominate satellite orbital error budgets and should be incorporated in future inferences.
