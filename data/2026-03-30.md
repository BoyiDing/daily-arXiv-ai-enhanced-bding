Abstract: The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) candidate event S251112cm suggests the presence of at least one compact object with sub-solar masses. Primordial black holes (PBHs), formed from the collapse of primordial density fluctuations in the early Universe, are a well-motivated candidate. We investigate the interpretation of S251112cm as the merger of two PBHs with masses in the range 0.1-1$\,M_\odot$. CNN+2D achieves smallest statistical uncertainties for multiple realizations at fiducial cosmology.

Abstract: Lightcone observations are the natural data format of galaxy surveys, but their evolving geometry breaks the translational symmetry assumed by standard convolutional neural networks (CNNs). ### [6] [Universality of Halo Shape and its Morphological Evolution across Cosmic Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26640)
*Ayan Nanda,Nishikanta Khandai,Jasjeet Singh Bagla,Swati Gavas*

Main category: astro-ph.CO

TL;DR: Dark matter halo shapes evolve toward more spherical forms over time, with median axis ratios following a universal curve as a function of peak height ν across different cosmologies and halo finders.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the evolution of dark matter halo shapes across different cosmological models and determine whether halo shape evolution follows universal scaling relations independent of cosmological parameters.

Method: Analyzed dark matter halo shapes in cosmological N-body simulations for both scale-free Einstein-De Sitter (EdS) and ΛCDM cosmologies. Computed axis ratios (q=b/a, s=c/a) using the shape tensor for well-resolved central halos. Used two different halo finding algorithms (SUBFIND and ROCKSTAR) for identification. Studied distributions P(q,s) and median values as functions of mass scaled by non-linear mass (M/M_nl) and peak height ν=δ_c/σ(M,z).

Result: Halo shapes become more spherical with decreasing redshift at fixed mass. Median axis ratios q(ν) and s(ν) follow a universal curve described by y=α-δ tanh[ω(log₁₀(ν)-μ)] across spectral indices n=-1.0 to n=-2.2. Results consistent for both SUBFIND and ROCKSTAR (with minor differences). Universality holds for ΛCDM runs, though with different small-ν behavior compared to scale-free models. Distribution widths can be reduced by classifying halos as oblate, triaxial, and prolate, each following universal behavior. Oblate halos increase in fraction over time at expense of other populations.

Conclusion: Dark matter halo shape evolution exhibits universal scaling with peak height ν across different cosmologies, suggesting fundamental physical processes govern halo shape evolution independent of specific cosmological parameters. The tanh function provides a good empirical description of this universal evolution.

Abstract: We investigate the evolution of dark matter halo shapes in cosmological N-body simulations both in scale free Einstein-De Sitter (EdS) and $Λ$CDM cosmologies. We compute the axis ratios ($q=b/a,s=c/a$) of well resolved central halos using the shape tensor. These halos are identified using two different halo finding algorithms, SUBFIND and ROCKSTAR. We find that at fixed mass, halos become more spherical with decreasing redshift. The distribution $P(q,s)$ along with their median values ($q$ and $s$) shows self-similar behaviour as a function of mass scaled by the non-linear mass, $(M/M_{nl})$ across power-law spectral indices for scale free EdS models. However the median $q$ and $s$ show a tighter self-similar evolution as a function of peak height $ν=δ_c/σ(M,z)$. We find that the median $q(ν)$ and $s(ν)$ are consistent with an evolution along a universal curve described by $y=α-δ\tanh \left[ ω\left(\log_{10}(ν) - μ\right)\right]$ across the spectral indices ranging from $n=-1.0$ to $n=-2.2$. Our results hold for both SUBFIND and ROCKSTAR, although there are some differences between them. The universality of the evolution of median $q(ν)$ and $s(ν)$ also holds for the $Λ$CDM runs, although with a different behaviour at small $ν$ compared to the scale free models. The width of the distributions of $P(q)$ and $P(s)$ in both, scale-free and $Λ$CDM, classes of simulations can be reduced further by classifying halos as oblate, triaxial and prolate, each of which also follows a universal behaviour. Although oblate halos are relatively rare at all redshifts, their fraction increases over time at the expense of the other two populations.
