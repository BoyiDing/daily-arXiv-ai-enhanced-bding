### [1] [Testing the cosmic distance-duality relation with localized fast radio bursts: a cosmological model-independent study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16869)
*Jéferson A. S. Fortunato,Surajit Kalita,Amanda Weltman*

Main category: astro-ph.CO

TL;DR: Testing cosmic distance-duality relation using SNIa luminosity distances and FRB angular-diameter distances reconstructed via neural networks, finding no significant deviations from CDDR.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To test the Etherington cosmic distance-duality relation (CDDR) using independent distance measurements from different astrophysical probes (SNIa and FRBs) to constrain potential violations of this fundamental cosmological relation.

Method: Data-driven reconstruction of angular-diameter distances from FRBs using artificial neural networks to infer smooth extragalactic dispersion-measure relation, then using its redshift derivative to recover H(z) and D_A^FRB(z) without parametric expansion history assumptions. Two complementary likelihood approaches: direct method using individual SNIa with full Pantheon+ covariance, and ML approach reconstructing SN Hubble diagram on FRB redshift grid with Monte Carlo and bootstrap uncertainty propagation.

Result: No statistically significant evidence for departures from CDDR at current precision. Both likelihood implementations give consistent posteriors. FRB reconstruction yields data-driven constraint on average host/near-source contribution DM_host = 128.8 ± 34.1 pc cm⁻³ (3σ confidence).

Conclusion: The cosmic distance-duality relation holds within current observational precision when tested with SNIa and FRB distance measurements, demonstrating the viability of FRBs as cosmological probes and the effectiveness of data-driven neural network approaches for distance reconstruction.

Abstract: We test the Etherington cosmic distance-duality relation (CDDR), by comparing Type Ia supernova (SNIa) luminosity-distance information from the Pantheon+ compilation with an angular-diameter-distance reconstructed from localized Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs). The core of our methodology is a data-driven reconstruction from FRBs using artificial neural networks (ANNs): we infer a smooth mean extragalactic dispersion-measure relation and use its redshift derivative to recover $H(z)$ and hence $D_\mathrm{A}^{\rm FRB}(z)$ without assuming a parametric form for the expansion history. Possible deviations from CDDR are parameterized through three one-parameter models of $η(z)\equiv D_\mathrm{L}/[(1+z)^2D_\mathrm{A}]$. We implement two complementary likelihoods: (i) a direct approach using individual SNIa with the full Pantheon+ covariance, and (ii) a machine-learning approach in which we reconstruct the SN Hubble diagram on the FRB redshift grid, propagating SN and FRB uncertainties into non-diagonal covariance matrices via Monte Carlo and bootstrap realizations. Within the FRB reconstruction, we anchor the mean extragalactic dispersion measure at $z=0$, which yields a data-driven constraint on the average host/near-source contribution $\mathrm{DM}_{\rm host}=128.8\pm 34.1\,\mathrm{pc\,cm^{-3}}$ ($3σ$ of statistical confidence). We find that both likelihood implementations give consistent posteriors and no statistically significant evidence for departures from CDDR at the current precision.

### [2] [Cosmic voids evolution in modified gravity via hydrodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17644)
*Tommaso Moretti,Noemi Frusciante,Giovanni Verza,Francesco Pace*

Main category: astro-ph.CO

TL;DR: The paper develops a hydrodynamical framework for spherical void evolution in modified gravity, applies it to luminal Galileon models, and uses void observables to constrain theory parameters.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To extend the description of spherical void evolution from General Relativity to modified gravity theories, providing a direct connection between model functions and observable void properties for constraining gravity modifications.

Method: Develops a hydrodynamical framework encoding gravity modifications into effective couplings in Euler and Poisson equations, yielding a non-linear evolution equation for Eulerian density contrast with time- and density-dependent effective gravitational strength. Applied to luminal Galileon models with Vainshtein screening.

Result: For viable parameters, finds hierarchy of MG effects: O(10%) modifications in gravitational couplings, percent-level shifts in void density evolution, and sub-percent deviations in Lagrangian-Eulerian mapping and shell-crossing thresholds. Shows analytically that voids remain in unscreened regime on physical branch.

Conclusion: The formalism provides a self-consistent approach to predict void dynamics and derive consistency constraints across a broad class of modified gravity models, with voids serving as probes of gravity modifications in underdense regions.

Abstract: We present a hydrodynamical description of spherical void evolution in modified gravity (MG), extending the standard General Relativity (GR) and dynamical dark energy treatment by encoding gravity modifications into effective couplings that enter the Euler and Poisson equations. This yields a compact non-linear evolution equation for the Eulerian density contrast, controlled by a time- and density-dependent effective gravitational strength, and provides a direct map between model functions and void observables. We apply the framework to the luminal Galileon class of models, where derivative self-interactions generate Vainshtein screening and might lead to a breakdown of the physical branch in sufficiently underdense regions. Exploiting this feature, we apply the void-informed viability requirement that translates into bounds on the theory parameter space and, equivalently, on the minimum attainable void depth as a function of redshift. For viable parameters of a concrete model, we quantify the impact of MG on isolated void evolution, the Lagrangian to Eulerian mapping, and the shell-crossing threshold. Relative to GR, we find a clear hierarchy of MG effects, with ${\cal O}(10\%)$ modifications in the gravitational couplings, percent-level shifts in the void density evolution, and sub-percent deviations in both the mapping and the shell-crossing thresholds. Moreover, within the adopted parametrization, we show analytically that voids always lie in an unscreened regime on the physical branch. Overall, the formalism provides a self-consistent route to predict void dynamics and consistency constraints in a broad class of MG models.
