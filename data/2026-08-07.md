### [1] [Summary statistic for pulsar timing arrays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05295)
*Gabriela Sato-Polito,Matias Zaldarriaga,Barak Zackay*

Main category: astro-ph.CO

TL;DR: The paper shows that gravitational-wave timing residuals for pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) can be fully characterized by an incoherent pulsar-term and a coherent Earth-term map on the sky. Since any signal yields the same Hellings-Downs angular correlation (decaying steeply with multipole), the signal is low-dimensional. The authors derive a PTA likelihood making these components explicit and use Fisher-matrix calculations on NANOGrav 15yr data to show that ℓ_max=2 of the Earth term and the monopole of the pulsar term retain ~95% of the signal information; for point sources, the dipole of the pulsar term is also needed.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To determine how few parameters are actually needed to fully characterize gravitational-wave signals observed by PTAs, given that the signal structure is inherently low-dimensional due to the steep multipole decay of the Hellings-Downs correlation.

Method: The authors decompose the PTA likelihood into explicit Earth-term map and pulsar-term variance components, then compute the Fisher matrix for both a stochastic background and a deterministic point source using the noise properties and sky locations of the NANOGrav 15yr dataset to quantify the information fraction retained as a function of spherical harmonic cutoff ℓ_max.

Result: For a stochastic background, ℓ_max=2 of the Earth-term map plus the monopole of the pulsar-term variance retain ~95% of the signal information. For a point source, including the dipole of the pulsar-term variance is additionally required to achieve a similar fraction.

Conclusion: Gravitational-wave signals in PTA data are inherently low-dimensional and can be efficiently parameterized with only a few spherical harmonic coefficients, which could simplify inference and enable more efficient searches and characterization of signals.

Abstract: The timing residuals produced by gravitational-wave signals can be described as an incoherent (pulsar term) contribution and a coherent (Earth term) map on the sky, which PTAs measure at the locations of the timed pulsars. The observed Earth term map and the variance induced by the pulsar term contain all of the information about any GW signal available to a PTA (assuming pulsar distances are unknown). Furthermore, any type of signal produces on average the same angular correlation function, the Hellings and Downs curve, which decays steeply with multipole as $C_\ell \propto 1/[(\ell+2)(\ell+1)\ell(\ell-1)]$. This suggests that the signal is inherently low-dimensional and therefore only a small number of parameters are needed to fully characterize it. We present an expression for the PTA likelihood that makes the dependence on the Earth term map and pulsar term variance explicit, and show that only a few spherical harmonic coefficients are needed to capture most of the information about the signal. To quantify this in a realistic setting, we compute the Fisher matrix of the amplitude of a stochastic background or a deterministic point source assuming the noise properties and sky locations of the pulsars in the NANOGrav 15yr dataset. We find that $\ell_{\rm max}=2$ of the Earth term map and the monopole of the pulsar term variance retain $\sim 95\%$ of the information about the signal. For a point source, including the dipole of the pulsar term variance is important to achieve a similar fraction.
